Method

  • Winner of year Y: highest calendar-year price return among stocks in the S&P 500 at the end of year Y (the convention used by S&P Dow Jones Indices and year-end press lists), requiring a full year of trading. So mid-year additions count (Tesla 2020, Palantir 2024) but SanDisk 2025 (spun off in February) does not.
  • Strategy A: on Jan 1 of each year put 100% in the previous year’s winner, hold 12 months, rotate.
  • Strategy B: two overlapping sleeves, each starting at $50. Each Jan 1, the sleeve whose two-year hold just ended sells and buys the previous year’s winner, holding it 24 months. At any time the portfolio holds the last two winners, without rebalancing between sleeves. (If the same stock wins twice, both sleeves hold it.)
  • Strategy C: on Jan 1 put 20% in each of the previous year’s top 5, hold 12 months, and rebalance to equal weight each year. The earliest start with complete, sourced top-5 lists every year is , using the 2008 list (no full top-5 list could be found for 1997, 1998, 2001, 2002 or 2004–2007). On the chart, C starts at the S&P 500’s value on that date. Its stats card uses $100 invested at its own start, and the “since” lines compare all four lines over the same window.
  • Acquired names inside a holding year (UST 2009, El Paso 2012, old Allergan 2015, Cablevision 2016, Warner Bros. Discovery 2026): valued at the actual deal consideration, with cash held at 0% for the rest of the year. Sources and the arithmetic are in momentum_top5.csv.
  • Benchmark: S&P 500 Total Return index (^SP500TR, dividends reinvested). Strategy returns are also total returns (dividend-adjusted closes). No costs, taxes or slippage.
  • Why 1999 and not 1998? Strategy B needs two winners from earlier years. On Jan 1 1998 that means the 1997 and 1996 winners, and 1996 is unverified. Strategy A (1997 winner Dell, +248% in 1998 per S&P DJI) and the S&P 500 could both start in 1998, but B could not without an assumption for its second half. The start date hasn’t been changed yet.
  • Window: the backtest starts -01-01, the first date both sleeves of Strategy B can be filled from verified winners (the 1996 winner could not be verified). It runs to the latest close; the last year is year-to-date. The chart uses year-end points, so max drawdown is measured on year-end values; intra-year drawdowns were deeper.
  • Delisted / acquired names (Dell, Reebok, Providian, Avaya, Family Dollar, XL Capital, Cabot Oil & Gas): for Strategies A and B, none was acquired or delisted inside its holding window. Their returns come from Exa’s per-ticker total-return pages (linked in the data file), with S&P DJI figures for the winning year where available.

Caveats

  • Hindsight and survivorship: membership comes from a point-in-time constituent list, but Yahoo has no prices for most delisted pre-2010 members, and ticker reuse corrupts some symbols (for example Yahoo’s “EP” is no longer El Paso). Pre-2010 winners therefore rely on published lists, and a few years are flagged.
  • Sensitive to definitions: with Jan-1 membership instead of year-end, several winners change (2024 would be Vistra, not Palantir; 2020 NVIDIA, not Tesla). Price and total-return rankings can also differ.
  • Very concentrated: A and B hold one or two stocks, with year-end drawdowns of about −85% to −97% in 2000–02. The result depends mostly on the start date. Since 2012 it looks much better than over the full period, which matches what the other 30-year backtest found.
  • This is not investment advice.
