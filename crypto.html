Method

  • Universe for year Y: the top 100 coins by market cap in CoinMarketCap’s Dec 31, Y snapshot, after removing stablecoins, wrapped/bridged tokens, liquid-staking receipts and gold-pegged tokens. A coin also needs a price in the Dec 31, Y−1 snapshot (a full year of history).
  • Volume screen: a coin whose Dec 31 trading volume was under 0.1% of its market cap is not used, because its price was not backed by trading (zero-volume tokens, or Bitcoin Classic’s +37 million% in 2023). Each excluded case is listed in the status tooltip and the CSV.
  • Prices: snapshots are taken at the end of the UTC day, so prices are Dec 31 UTC closes in USD. The last point is the latest full UTC day.
  • Strategy A: on Jan 1 put 100% in last year’s #1 coin, hold 12 months, rotate. Strategy B: 20% in each of last year’s top 5, rebalanced every Jan 1.
  • Dead coins: a holding that leaves later snapshots is valued from CoinMarketCap’s daily history, or from the last weekly snapshot that still listed it. Triggers (TRIG, a 2017 top-5 coin) was dropped in Oct 2018 after falling 98%, and is valued there. Most picks did not die but fell 90–98% the next year anyway (Verge 2018, GALA 2022, MANTRA 2025).
  • Benchmarks: Bitcoin buy-and-hold from the same snapshots. The S&P 500 total return index (from the Momentum tab’s data) is shown for reference. No fees, slippage or exchange risk. Many early coins could only be bought on small exchanges.
  • 2015 is the first winner year: CoinMarketCap’s first Dec 31 snapshot is 2013, but before 2015 the top 100 held few coins with a full year of history and liquid markets. So backtests start Jan 1, 2016, using 2015’s winner.

Caveats

  • Selection at year-end: like the S&P page, the winner is picked from the year-end top 100, so it includes coins that only reached the top 100 because they went up. That is the point of the strategy, but it also selects small, thin tokens.
  • Single source: year-by-year prices come from CoinMarketCap. Winners were cross-checked against CoinLore’s yearly lists and press roundups where possible, and the status column says which ones were.
  • Extremely concentrated: Strategy A lost about 99.99% of its peak value. A $100 stake was worth about $ at its best and well under $1 later. Results swing wildly with the start year; try the slider.
  • This is not investment advice.
